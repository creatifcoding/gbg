## Transferable Principles from IUPAC, Linnaeus, DSM, and PhyloCode

### Principle 1: The Name Should Mirror the Structure (IUPAC)

IUPAC organic nomenclature succeeds because the compositional semantics of the name parallels the compositional structure of the molecule. Mathematical structures are also compositionally defined — a concept is built from properties, operations, and base structures. The name should encode that composition.

### Principle 2: Systematic Flexibility, Not Pure Rigor (IUPAC)

The Geneva Congress (1892) tried pure rigor. It failed — names were too long and cumbersome. The Liège Nomenclature (1930) succeeded by allowing principled exceptions. Mathemes must do the same: systematic names for clarity, accepted short forms for efficiency.

### Principle 3: Editorial Adoption Drives Change (IUPAC)

IUPAC won because journal editors and reference-work compilers adopted it. For Mathemes, adoption requires buy-in from textbook authors and mathematical publishers. The dual-register approach lowers the barrier: systematic names appear in parentheses alongside traditional names, gradually building familiarity.

### Principle 4: Hierarchy Should Be Visible (Linnaeus)

Linnaean binomial nomenclature encodes taxonomic position. Mathematical naming should encode structural hierarchy: a Hilbert space is a Banach space is a normed space is a metric space is a topological space. The name "complete inner-product space" reveals this hierarchy; "Hilbert space" conceals it.

### Principle 5: Reform Requires Institutional Authority (DSM)

The DSM-III rename of "manic depression" to "bipolar disorder" succeeded because the American Psychiatric Association had the authority to mandate it. Mathematics has no equivalent body with nomenclature authority. The International Mathematical Union (IMU) or the AMS could potentially fill this role, but the political will would need to be built.

### Principle 6: Dual Naming Is the Practical Path (PhyloCode)

PhyloCode's mixed results show that wholesale replacement of established names fails. Dual naming — systematic name alongside traditional name — is the adoption strategy that works. Mathemes should always provide both.

### Principle 7: It Takes a Generation (All)

IUPAC: 38 years from Geneva to Liège. Linnaeus: decades to widespread adoption. DSM: gradual over multiple editions. Nomenclature reform is generational. Mathemes should plan accordingly — the goal is to seed a system that others extend, not to rename all of mathematics overnight.
