## 2. EMSO Doctrine (Electromagnetic Spectrum Operations)

### 2.1 JP 3-85: Joint Electromagnetic Spectrum Operations

JP 3-85 is the foundational joint doctrine publication for EMSO. It provides guidance on planning, executing, and assessing joint electromagnetic spectrum operations.

**Key definitions from JP 3-85:**
- **Electromagnetic Spectrum Operations (EMSO)**: Coordinated military actions to exploit, attack, protect, and manage the electromagnetic environment
- **Electromagnetic Operating Environment (EMOE)**: The resulting product of the power and time distribution, in various frequency ranges, of the radiated or conducted electromagnetic emission levels that may be encountered by a military force, system, or platform
- **The modern EMOE is "congested, contested, and constrained" (3C)**

**Source:** [JP 3-85](https://irp.fas.org/doddir/dod/jp3_85.pdf), [JCS Portal](https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/pubs/jp3_85.pdf)

### 2.2 Three Pillars of Electronic Warfare

EW remains the core combat discipline within EMSO, organized into three pillars:

| Pillar | Abbreviation | Definition | Tsingou Relevance |
|--------|-------------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Electronic Attack (EA)** | EA | Use of electromagnetic energy, directed energy, or anti-radiation weapons to attack personnel, facilities, or equipment for degrading, neutralizing, or destroying enemy combat capability | Tsingou monitors EA effects as observable signals; visualizes jamming patterns |
| **Electronic Protection (EP)** | EP | Actions taken to protect personnel, facilities, and equipment from any effects of friendly or enemy use of the EMS that degrade, neutralize, or destroy friendly combat capability | Tsingou detects EP signatures; maps friendly emission patterns |
| **Electronic Warfare Support (ES)** | ES | Actions tasked by, or under direct control of, an operational commander to search for, intercept, identify, and locate or localize sources of intentional and unintentional radiated electromagnetic energy | **Primary Tsingou domain** — ES is the SIGINT-relevant pillar |

**Critical distinction: ES vs. SIGINT**
- **ES** responds to immediate operational requirements; tasked by operational commanders
- **SIGINT** provides both near-real-time ES-derived intelligence AND long-term operational, scientific, and technical intelligence
- ES is tactical; SIGINT spans tactical through strategic
- Tsingou supports both: real-time ES through live adapter streams, SIGINT through historical analysis (JetStream replay)

**Source:** [EMSOPEDIA EW](https://www.emsopedia.org/entries/electronic-warfare-aka-electromagnetic-warfare/), [AFDP 3-85](https://www.doctrine.af.mil/Portals/61/documents/AFDP_3-85/AFDP%203-85%20Electromagnetic%20Spectrum%20Ops.pdf)

### 2.3 EMSO as Broader Framework

EMSO encompasses more than traditional EW. It integrates:
- Electronic Warfare (EA + EP + ES)
- Signals Intelligence (SIGINT)
- Spectrum Management Operations (SMO)
- Cyber Electromagnetic Activities (CEMA)
- Information Operations (IO)

The doctrinal evolution from "EW" to "EMSO" reflects recognition that the electromagnetic spectrum is a warfighting domain, not merely a support function.

**Terminological shift**: "Electronic" → "Electromagnetic" (EMS, EMA, EMP) reflects the broader scope of modern operations.

**Source:** [EMSOPEDIA EMSO](https://www.emsopedia.org/entries/electro-magnetic-spectrum-operation-emso/)

### 2.4 DoD EMS Superiority Strategy (2020)

Released October 2020, builds on 2013 DoD EMS Strategy and 2017 DoD EW Strategy.

**Vision**: "Freedom of Action in the Electromagnetic Spectrum — at the time, place, and parameters of our choosing."

**Five interdependent goals:**
1. Develop superior EMS capabilities
2. Evolve to an agile, fully integrated EMS infrastructure
3. Pursue total force readiness in the EMS
4. Secure enduring partnerships for EMS advantage
5. Establish effective EMS governance

**EMBM (Electromagnetic Battle Management)** defined as:
- Comprehensive framework for dynamic monitoring, assessing, planning, directing operations in EMS
- Leverages trusted data sources for EMS situational awareness and decision support
- Interfaces with systems/networks (including broadband and software-defined)
- Must provide dynamic control of real-time operations via machine-machine and human-machine collaboration

**Tsingou alignment**: EMBM's requirements for real-time monitoring, dynamic assessment, trusted data sources, and visualization map directly to Tsingou's d2ts pipeline, NATS fabric, and 4-layer rendering.

**Source:** [DoD EMS Strategy 2020](https://media.defense.gov/2020/Oct/29/2002525927/-1/-1/0/ELECTROMAGNETIC_SPECTRUM_SUPERIORITY_STRATEGY.PDF), [STRATCOM Implementation](https://www.stratcom.mil/Media/News/News-Article-View/Article/2723717/)

### 2.5 Joint Electromagnetic Spectrum Operations Center (JEC)

Established July 2023 under U.S. Strategic Command. The JEC:
- Serves as key piece of Pentagon's spectrum superiority strategy implementation
- Raises readiness of joint force within the electromagnetic spectrum
- Coordinates EMS actions across functional staff elements and joint force components

**Source:** [DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2023/07/25/strategic-command-officially-creates-joint-electromagnetic-spectrum-operations-center/)

## 3. CEMA (Cyber Electromagnetic Activities)

### 3.1 FM 3-12: Cyberspace Operations and Electromagnetic Warfare

FM 3-12 (August 2021 revision) is the Army's doctrine for CEMA. It defines CEMA as:

> "The process of planning, integrating, and synchronizing cyberspace operations and electronic warfare in support of unified land operations."

**CEMA = CO + EW + SMO**
- **Cyberspace Operations (CO)**: Offensive and defensive operations in cyberspace
- **Electronic Warfare (EW)**: EA + EP + ES
- **Spectrum Management Operations (SMO)**: Frequency assignment, interference resolution, spectrum planning

**Proponent**: U.S. Army Cyber Center of Excellence (Fort Eisenhower)

**Source:** [FM 3-12](https://irp.fas.org/doddir/army/fm3-12.pdf)

### 3.2 FM 3-38: Cyber Electromagnetic Activities (Legacy)

FM 3-38 (2014) was the original CEMA doctrine, superseded by FM 3-12 but still referenced for foundational concepts. It established the CEMA framework as integrating CO, EW, and SMO under unified command.

**Source:** [FM 3-38](https://irp.fas.org/doddir/army/fm3-38.pdf)

### 3.3 CEMA Cell Structure

At division and above, a CEMA cell within the operations center:
- Plans and synchronizes cyber and EW effects
- Coordinates spectrum management
- Integrates CEMA into the Military Decision Making Process (MDMP)
- Coordinates with the JEMSOC at joint level
- Manages the EW Working Group (EWWG)

**Tsingou relevance**: Tsingou's Session Configuration (Direction phase from ADR-010) maps to the CEMA cell's planning function — defining collection requirements, analysis parameters, and deconfliction rules.

### 3.4 CEMA in Multi-Domain Operations (MDO)

MDO is the Army's transformational concept for 2030+:
- EMS is one of 5 domains (land, maritime, air, space, cyberspace) plus the EMS
- CEMA enables cross-domain synergy through convergence of cyber and EW effects
- Multi-Domain Effects Platoon (MDEP) brings EW capabilities to brigade level
- MDO demands rapid sensor-to-shooter chains that cross domain boundaries

**Key challenge**: Spectrum deconfliction across joint forces in contested environments

**Tsingou alignment**: Tsingou's multi-source ingestion (8 adapters) and cross-source correlation (d2ts join) directly address the MDO requirement for converging multi-domain sensor data.

**Source:** [Multi-Domain Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-Domain_Operations), [Amentum CEMA](https://www.amentum.com/news/ensuring-cyber-and-electromagnetic-effects-integration-for-multi-domain-operations/)

### 3.5 JADC2 Integration

Joint All-Domain Command and Control (JADC2) aims to connect sensors to shooters across all domains:
- CEMA provides EMS effects integration for JADC2
- Spectrum deconfliction is critical in contested environments
- Indo-Pacific Command has focused JADC2 on higher-level spectrum deconfliction
- Cultural shifts needed alongside technology integration

**Source:** [AFCEA](https://www.afcea.org/signal-media/defense-operations/joint-electronic-warfare-cyber-and-spectrum-operations-need-work), [GovConWire](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/whats-next-for-cjadc2-in-2024)

## 4. TPED Processing Chain

### 4.1 TPED Cycle Definition

TPED (Tasking, Processing, Exploitation, and Dissemination) is the ISR operational cycle:

| Phase | Definition | Military Function |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Tasking** | Directing new collection based on intelligence requirements | Priority Intelligence Requirements (PIR), Collection Management |
| **Processing** | Converting raw data to exploitable form | Signal conditioning, demodulation, protocol decoding, format conversion |
| **Exploitation** | Reviewing processed data to create derived information | Analysis, pattern recognition, entity extraction, threat assessment |
| **Dissemination** | Delivering derived information to end users for decision-making | Intelligence reports, alerts, COP updates, feeds to C2 systems |

**Feedback loop**: End-user feedback drives new tasking, closing the cycle.

### 4.2 TPED → Tsingou Mapping

| TPED Phase | Tsingou Subsystem | Implementation |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Tasking** | Session Configuration (Direction) | Define adapters, priority sources, focus areas, ATT&CK techniques |

| **Dissemination** | OutputBridge + STIX/TAXII export | Atom state → rendering layers, NATS fan-out, STIX bundles, alerts |

## 5. Electronic Order of Battle (EOB)

### 5.1 Definition

The Electronic Order of Battle (EOB) is an advanced exploitation capability designed to:
- **Identify** electromagnetic emitters
- **Localize** emitter positions (DF, TDOA, FDOA)
- **Track** emitter movement over time
- **Characterize** emitter technical parameters (frequency, modulation, power, antenna pattern)

EOB covers both COMINT (communications intelligence) and ELINT (electronic intelligence) emitters.

### 5.2 EOB Data Elements

An EOB entry typically includes:
- Emitter type/platform association
- Operating frequency bands
- Modulation characteristics
- Antenna parameters
- Geolocation (with confidence ellipse)
- First/last observed timestamps
- Association with military units or weapon systems
- Threat assessment (hostile, friendly, neutral, unknown)

### 5.3 EOB → Tsingou Mapping

| EOB Element | Tsingou Equivalent |
|------------|-------------------|
| Emitter record | `BaseSignal` with `kind: "sdr"` + `SdrSignal` extension |
| Frequency/modulation | Signal payload fields (frequency, bandwidth, modulation) |
| Geolocation | Metadata with lat/lon/alt/confidence from DF/TDOA processing |
| Threat characterization | Derived graph output → `anomalyAtom`, STIX indicator |
| Unit association | Cross-source correlation (d2ts join with OSINT/HUMINT feeds) |
| Temporal tracking | d2ts versioning (multi-dimensional) + JetStream history |
| Database | `SchemaRegistry` (NATS KV) + `tsingou-schemas` bucket |

## 7. Geolocation Techniques

### 7.1 Direction Finding (DF)

- Provides bearing to transmission using angle from sensor to transmitter
- Requires fewer sensors (1-2 DF arrays)
- Accuracy degrades linearly with range
- Well-suited for space-constrained deployments

### 7.2 Time Difference of Arrival (TDOA)

- Pinpoints geographic coordinates using time differences between 3+ receivers
- Requires precise time synchronization and station location knowledge
- Less range-dependent than DF
- Higher accuracy but requires more infrastructure

### 7.3 Frequency Difference of Arrival (FDOA)

- Uses Doppler shift differences measured by moving receivers
- Requires relative motion between stations and emitter
- Particularly effective with satellite-based collection
- Often combined with TDOA for hybrid T/FDOA geolocation

### 7.4 Relevance to Tsingou

Tsingou's SDR adapter pipeline (ADR-011) ingests geolocation data from external DF/TDOA/FDOA processors. The d2ts derived graph can:
- Correlate position fixes across multiple sensors
- Apply temporal windowing for track maintenance
- Feed geolocation data to the R3F 3D rendering layer for spatial visualization
- Cross-reference with OSINT/STIX data for entity resolution

**Source:** [CRFS DF Techniques](https://www.crfs.com/blog/radio-direction-finding-techniques-and-applications-for-ew-and-sigint/), [CRFS DF & Geolocation](https://www.crfs.com/solutions/df-geolocation)

## 8. EW Reprogramming and EWIRDB

### 8.1 EWIR Process

Electronic Warfare Integrated Reprogramming (EWIR) consists of four processes:
1. **Change Detection** — Identify changes in threat technical capabilities
2. **Impact Determination** — Assess how changes affect friendly EW systems
3. **Reprogramming** — Update mission data, missionware, OFPs, or hardware
4. **Change Delivery** — Distribute reprogrammed data to fielded systems

Overall process code name: **PACER WARE**

### 8.2 EWIRDB

The Electronic Warfare Integrated Reprogramming Database (EWIRDB):
- Primary DoD-approved source for technical parametric data on non-communications electronic emitters
- Only DoD-validated source of emitter intelligence
- Contains emitter characteristics: frequency, power, modulation, pulse parameters, antenna patterns
- Used for mission data file generation and threat library updates

### 8.3 Rapid Reprogramming Challenges

Ukraine conflict lessons learned (2024):
- EW landscape changes between 3 weeks and 3 months
- Traditional reprogramming cycles too slow for near-peer conflict
- Army conducting data pilot for rapid EW reprogramming
- Need for automated change detection and validation

### 8.4 Tsingou as Reprogramming Accelerator

Tsingou's architecture aligns with rapid reprogramming needs:
- **SchemaRegistry** (NATS KV) = dynamic threat library (runtime-updateable)
- **d2ts derived graph** = automated change detection (anomaly operators)
- **Hot-plug adapters** = rapid sensor integration
- **STIX interop** = standardized threat data exchange
- Historical analysis via JetStream = baseline comparison

**Source:** [EWIRDB](https://www.globalsecurity.org/intell/systems/ewirdb.htm), [DefenseScoop Rapid Reprogramming](https://defensescoop.com/2024/05/10/army-electronic-warfare-data-pilot-rapid-reprogramming/)

## 10. Spectrum Situational Awareness

### 10.1 Recognized Electromagnetic Picture (REP)

The REP is the EMS equivalent of the Common Operating Picture (COP). It provides:
- Real-time visualization of the electromagnetic environment
- Friendly and hostile emitter locations
- Spectrum occupancy maps
- Interference and jamming indicators
- Integration with other ISR data (ground, overhead, blue force tracking)

### 10.2 Display Requirements

Per EMSO doctrine, spectrum SA displays must support:
- Frequency vs. time (spectrogram/waterfall)
- Frequency vs. amplitude (real-time spectrum)
- Geographic overlay (emitter locations on map)
- Temporal trending (signal pattern evolution)
- Alerting (threshold breaches, new emitter detection)

### 10.3 Tsingou 4-Layer Mapping to REP

| Rendering Layer | REP Component | Display Type |
|----------------|---------------|-------------|
| **R3F (z:0)** | Geographic emitter overlay | 3D spatial — emitter positions, signal propagation |
| **visx (z:1)** | Spectrum analytics | SVG — spectrograms, frequency distributions, correlation plots |
| **p5 (z:2)** | Real-time spectrum | Canvas — waterfall display, FFT magnitude, constellation diagrams |
| **DOM (z:3)** | Operator controls + alerts | Text — thresholds, adapter config, alert notifications, annotation |

This mapping directly satisfies the REP requirements:
- Frequency × time → p5 waterfall
- Frequency × amplitude → p5 FFT display
- Geographic overlay → R3F 3D scene
- Temporal trending → visx time-series
- Alerting → DOM alert atoms
